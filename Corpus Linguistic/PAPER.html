The application of corpus linguistics in analysing the Coronavirus corpus

Author

Phuong Dang

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to apply the method of corpus linguistics to the Coronavirus corpus. We will address the research question: how did people change their views about the Corona pandemic from before to after the lockdown. At different time points, our views about a certain event might be different. It is in the best interest of corpus linguistics to explore the impacts of a particular event on society, economy, and culture. The major statistical method conducted in the paper is correspondence analysis (CA). CA is used to analyze the dependence between two (or more) categorical variables. The categorical variables in corpus linguistics are words, text, phrases, etc. CA then will visualize categories of the variables as points in a low-dimension space to interpret the relationship between/among these variables. We, therefore, aim to see if there are existing varieties in the texts and words used before and after the lockdown.

4 Methodology

4.1 Frequency analysis

The first corpus technique is frequency analysis. In this approach, we will explore the frequency of words. The subcorpus will be converted to vectors and from there the term frequence of the words or texts can be found (Baron, Rayson, and Archer 2009). The most frequently occurring words will be visualized by the bar plots.

4.2 Keyword analysis

Secondly, we apply the Keyword Analysis (KA). Keywords are not essentially the words that appear most frequently in the corpus, but they are words identified by statistical comparison between the ‘target’ corpus the ‘reference’ corpus. In particular, we want to see if the word appears more often in the target corpus as compared to its occurrence in the reference corpus. If yes, the word may be a key term of the target corpus. A keyword list consists items (e.g., texts, words) that are either significantly frequent (positive keywords) or infrequent (negative keywords), and is a useful starting point for much linguist analysis (Scott et al. 2001).

The keyword analysis is based on the association scores and it regards three criteria:

  • A frequency of three or higher in the target corpus.

  • A PMI score of two or higher means that the probability of the keyword in the target context is minimum four time higher than its probability in the data taken altogether.

  • A signed G score of two or higher such that higher G^2 means there is more evidence that two words are dependent but do not distinguish between attraction.

In KA, we choose the file of January of 2020 as the target corpus, and the remaining corpora including files of February, March, April, and May 2020 as the reference corpus. Since January 2020 was when Corona virus appeared, we choose the corpus of January as a target text, and from there we tend to find which keywords are the best to target and provide valuable insights.

4.3 Correspondence analysis

Thirdly, we perform CA, which is the major method in this paper. CA is a discovery technique mainly used to explore complex categorical data by identifying patterns of association and disassociation of those data. This technique makes more sense if there are so many differences between configurations (Glynn 2014). By doing CA we investigate how the observed counts in the contingency table deviate from expected counts under statistical independence.

Methodologically, CA produces the contingency table which indicates the difference between the rows and columns of frequencies. Next, it takes the frequency of co-occurring features and then converts them into distances. Particularly, CA computes the eigenvectors of a correlation matrix and generates the Euclidean map. Those vectors will then be plotted into two or three dimensions through a contingency plot (also called biplot). The plot visualizes how things are linked together by distance or proximity.

In more detail in the method of CA, in a contingency table, there are columns and rows containing all the relative frequencies of co-occurrence frequencies that appear simultaneously. Row profiles and column profiles can be created using the same process. For example, row profiles can be calculated by adding up the number of occurrences for each feature in a row, and then dividing each of those occurrences by their sum. The same step applies to column calculation and will give the column profiles. Note that not all co-occurrences are equal in importance, for example, some infrequency features might affect the co-occurrences. Therefore, CA might use the term “mass” (similarly named “weight”) to calculate the average score and resolve certain biases (Oleg and Greenacre 2007; Greenacre 2010). The mathematics to get the row profile and column profile are as follows:

  • Suppose two categorical variables X and Y have an n_r * n_c cross-table with:

    • F_{ij} the observed count in cell (i,j)

    • F_{i+}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_c} F_{ij} the marginal row count for row i

    • F_{+j} = \sum_{i=1}^{n_r} F_{ij} the marginal column count for column j

    • n= \sum_{i=1}^{n_r} \sum_{j=1}^{n_c} F_{ij} total count

Furthermore, the distance matrix of CA can be generated by conducting the Chi-squared distance measure between two rows i and i^* or between two columns j and j^*. The p-value from the Chi-squared test can notice us that CA is or is not statistically significant to fit the data.

  • The strength of the relationship between X and Y can be measured using the Pearson-Chi square statistic: \chi^2 = \sum_{i=1}^{n_r} \sum_{j=1}^{n_c}\frac{(F_{ij} - E_{ij})^2}{E_{ij}} with E_{ij} = \frac{F_{i+}F_{+j}}{n} the expected count if X and Y are statistically independent.

  • If the Pearson-Chi square test indicates that X and Y are statistically dependent, it is relevant to use CA to further study the nature of the association between X and Y.

Additionally, inertia is the term used in CA to illustrate the degree of variation. The inertia is calculated based on observed and expected co-occurrence frequencies. High inertia is when column and row profiles have large deviations from their averages. In CA, we investigate the dissimilarity between the profiles, so the higher gain inertia is preferred.

Moreover, in the biplot, two dimensions of the plot represent a certain percentage in the structure of the data variation. These interpreted variance percentages can be seen in the x-axes and y-axes of the biplot.

After loading the corpora, we create some functions to perform the CA. The first function is compile_frequencies such that rows correspond to features, and columns correspond to files. In the map function, we produce a frequency list for each filename of the features we are interested in. We combine all files in one data frame. # Function complile_frequencies
compile_frequencies <- function(features,
                                fnames,
                                short_fnames,
                                ...) {
  d <- map(setNames(fnames, short_fnames), function(fname) {
    freqlist(fname, ...)[features]
  }) %>%
  bind_cols() %>% 
  data.frame(row.names = features) %>% 
  as.matrix() %>% 
  t() %>% 
  drop_empty_rc()
}

# Function get_coords
get_coords <- function(d_ca, sub_corp) {
  texts_df <- row_pcoord(d_ca)[,c(1, 2)] %>% 
    as_tibble(rownames = "text") %>% 
    mutate(Subcorpus = sub_corp)
  
  words_df <- col_pcoord(d_ca)[,c(1, 2)] %>% 
    as_tibble(rownames = "word")
  
  list(
    rows = texts_df,
    cols = words_df
  )
}

5.4 Remarks

In the first statistical approach, frequency analysis, we can see the highest occurrence frequency of 20 words in the corpus for March and April. Some of the most popular words are coronavirus, covid, now, time, public, government, people, health, new. Because these words mainly reflect the context of the Coronavirus, so it is clear that it has a high probability of appearing in the corpus. In the analysis of keywords, we do not focus on the frequency of occurrence of words, but often tend to compare the target corpus and the reference corpus. The result of KA all satisfies the three criteria of Frequency, PMI, and Signed G^2.

In CA, the first step is to evaluate whether there is a significant dependency between the rows and columns. We used the chi-square statisitc for examining the association between row and column variables. Since p-value of Pearson Chi-squared test is statistically significant, so we rejected the null hypothesis (H_0) and concluded that applying CA is meaningful to investigate the association between row and column profiles. Moreover, in CA, we applied n-gram techniques. First n-gram is Unigram in which we used the content words as features for CA. Based on biplot, we could see special words such as break, covid-19, pandemic, lockdown. Next, we made the second n-gram as Bigram. In this n-gram, the text is the combinations of two words, so the size of frequency list can be less than a unigram (e.g., only one word). Finally, we proceed to another n-gram called Trigram. In this n-gram, the text (item) is the combinations of three words, and obviously, the size of frequency list of word combinations is smaller than bi-grams or unigram. Regarding the n-gram approaches in CA, more than 90\% of the variation is explained by the first two dimensions. Based on the results and discussions of these methods, we illustrate that there are dissimilarities in the words and texts used in the corpora of before and after lockdown because two subcorpus were separated by first two dimensions. We, therefore, can state that the views of people about the covid pandemic have changed when lockdown happened.

6 Conclusion

In this paper, we explore the change in people’s viewpoint about the corona pandemic. Through various statistical techniques, we generate the frequency lists of words, the keywords, and the correspondence analysis for investigating varieties. Based on the result of our analysis, we conclude that human views have changed since the coronavirus lockdown. However, in order to firmly emphasize the impacts of coronavirus in our lives from the beginning of the pandemic to the present, further investigations such as using a larger corpus, or taking other approaches may be necessary like collocation, logistic regression, and clustering analysis.
